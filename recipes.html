Main · Vegetarian

Slow-Cooked Dal Makhani

Black lentils simmered overnight, finished with a spoon of Garuda Masala at the very end to keep its aroma intact.

Ingredients

  • 1 cupWhole black urad dal, soaked overnight
  • ¼ cupRajma (kidney beans), soaked overnight
  • 2 tbspGhee or unsalted butter
  • 1 tspCumin seeds
  • 2 tspGaruda Masala (our signature blend)
  • ½ cupFresh cream, plus extra for serving
  • to tasteSalt and a pinch of sugar

Method

i.

Drain the soaked dal and rajma. Pressure cook with 4 cups of water and a pinch of salt for 8–10 whistles until completely soft.

ii.

In a heavy pot, heat the ghee and add cumin seeds. Once they crackle, add the cooked dal and mash lightly with the back of a spoon.

iii.

Simmer on low for 45 minutes, stirring occasionally. The dal should darken and thicken — this is the whole point of the recipe.

iv.

Take off the heat. Stir in the Garuda Masala and cream. Serve immediately with a swirl of extra cream on top.

— Rukmini's note: add the masala last —

Technique Note

Why we add the masala off the heat.

Most recipes tell you to add spice while the pot is bubbling. We do the opposite. Ground spices lose their volatile oils above 180°F — the same oils that give our blends their distinctive aroma.

Stir them in after you've turned off the flame and the residual heat does the work. Your kitchen will smell different. Your dish will taste the way we intended.

— a small rule —

This is the single most important habit in our kitchen. It applies to every recipe on this page.

Step 01

Toast the whole

Before anything is ground, we toast whole spices in a dry pan. You'll hear them pop — that's the signal to stop.

Step 02

Grind in small batches

Stone-ground spices keep their texture and heat. We never grind more than a week's worth in advance.

Step 03

Add after the flame

The final gesture. Stir the masala in when the pot is still hot but no longer bubbling — and cover it for two minutes before serving.

Cook's Notes

A letter from the kitchen.

"

To the cook who just opened this page,

Recipe writing is not like other writing. It assumes the reader is standing in a kitchen with dirty hands, one eye on the pot, and no patience for extra words. So we try to write recipes like we'd want to receive them — short, precise, and honest about where the tricky parts are.

Three things to know before you start:

1. Salt early, spice late. Salt is a building block. Spice is a finish. Adding them at the wrong time is the most common reason a home dish tastes flat.

2. Use a heavy pot. This is not a snob thing. Thin pots burn at the base before the top is ready. A cheap heavy-bottomed pan beats an expensive thin one.

3. Taste as you go. A recipe is a direction, not a law. If your tomatoes are sweeter than ours, use less jaggery. Trust your tongue.

And if something doesn't work — write to us. Every batch of our blends has been adjusted because a customer wrote in about a dish they made.

From the workshop

Meera & Arjun
